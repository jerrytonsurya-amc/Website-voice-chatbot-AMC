The global environment through CY2025 remained challenging and unusually uncertain,
shaped by a confluence of geopolitical and policy-driven risks:
Against this backdrop, risk appetite for emerging markets remained episodic and highly
sensitive to global liquidity conditions.
 US trade policy uncertainty resurfaced as tariffs increasingly became tools of negotiation
rather than instruments of long-term economic strategy, contributing to volatility in global
trade flows and capital markets.
 Geopolitical tensions in the Middle East continued to pose risks to energy markets and
global supply chains.
 India–Pakistan tensions, following the horrific terrorist attacks in Pahalgam and the
subsequent skirmish in May, added a regional risk premium to Indian assets, albeit
temporarily.
 The ongoing Russia–Ukraine conflict remained unresolved, prolonging disruptions in
global commodities, energy markets, and geopolitical alignments.
Global Backdrop: Heightened Uncertainty, Persistent Fragilities
Indian equity markets experienced a year of consolidation and relative underperformance,
particularly when viewed against select emerging and developed markets.
 Moderation in credit growth, which in turn weighed on consumption, especially
discretionary spending.
 Valuation concerns, particularly in mid- and small-cap segments, led to India trailing
several EM and DM peers on a relative basis.
 Artificial Intelligence as a market driver became increasingly dominant globally. The
lack of listed, scalable AI-native beneficiaries in India, combined with concerns that AI
could structurally disrupt traditional IT services, dampened investor sentiment toward
the Indian IT sector and, by extension, the broader market.
 Failure to conclude key trade agreements further exacerbated foreign equity outflows
during parts of the year.
 Large FDI repatriations via marquee IPOs such as Hyundai, ICICI Prudential AMC, and
LG Electronics led to sustained equity supply and incremental pressure on the Indian
Rupee.
 Toward the latter part of the year, large-cap stocks staged a strong recovery, hitting new
highs, while mid- and small-cap indices continued to lag, constrained by still-elevated
valuations.

 Financials: We continue to selectively increase exposure to segments benefiting from
India’s deepening financialisation. This includes life insurance and asset management,
where long-term penetration remains low and industry structure favours scaled,
well-capitalised players with strong distribution. Improving product mix, rising equity
participation by households, and steady regulatory support underpin our conviction in
this space.
 Consumer Platforms: Within consumption, we are reallocating capital toward platform-led
models that benefit from structural shifts in consumer behaviour. This includes exposure
to digital consumption ecosystems where scale, data advantages, and optionality across
adjacencies offer long runway growth. At the same time, we remain cautious on traditional
consumer incumbents where quick commerce and changing distribution dynamics are
structurally compressing margins and moats. Our consumer exposure is therefore
increasingly selective and skewed toward underpenetrated categories with clear volume-led
growth rather than pricing power alone.
 Healthcare: Structural Growth with Selective Innovation Healthcare remains a core
structural allocation. We are incrementally adding exposure to specialised healthcare
delivery and pharmaceuticals where demographic tailwinds, rising healthcare intensity,
and expanding therapeutic breadth support sustained earnings growth. Within pharma,
we favour companies moving beyond legacy therapy concentration into emerging
segments with higher growth optionality. In hospitals, we prefer differentiated platforms
with strong clinical depth and expansion into high-growth niches.
 Infrastructure, Power, and Electrification Themes: We maintain conviction in power
generation, transmission, and related infrastructure. As electricity demand continues to
compound and the generation mix evolves, peak power deficits are likely to re-emerge,
necessitating sustained investments across the grid. Allied to this, we see structural
opportunity in electrical equipment and cables, supported by power capex, urban real
estate recovery, and rapid data centre build-out.
 Industrials, Metals, and Domestic Capex: Our exposure to metals and industrials is
anchored in India’s urbanisation and manufacturing push. Demand visibility remains
healthy, supported by favourable input cost dynamics, policy safeguards, and infrastructure
spending. We remain selective, focusing on balance sheet strength and cost competitiveness.
 Energy and Autos: Cyclical Upside with Discipline: Oil Marketing Companies continue
to benefit from benign crude prices and a relatively stable pricing regime. In automobiles,
multiple cyclical tailwinds—potential GST rationalisation, improving credit growth, a
gradual recovery in urban discretionary demand, and prospective central government
wage revisions—support a more constructive outlook.
 Asset Allocation and Risk Management: Across the portfolio, we retain a clear tilt toward
large caps where valuation comfort has improved and earnings durability remains superior.
Within the Multi-Asset Fund, we have gradually reduced exposure to gold and silver, as
recent price action increasingly resembles late-stage commodity behaviour, notwithstanding
supportive long-term fundamentals.
Our portfolio positioning reflects a balance between cyclical opportunities and long-duration
structural themes, while remaining disciplined on valuation and increasingly biased toward
large-cap businesses with superior earnings visibility.
Overall, portfolio changes during the period reflect a bottom-up reallocation toward sectors
where structural growth, improving industry economics, and valuation discipline intersect,
while trimming exposures where competitive intensity or late-cycle dynamics are rising.

Outlook for CY2026: Cautious Optimism with Selective Opportunities
 Fiscal initiatives such as income tax rationalization, potential GST cuts, continued

reforms are expected to accelerate growth.

Policy Tailwinds
 Equity markets, especially Mid and Small caps continue to trade at demanding multiples
versus historical averages, while earnings estimates have only recently begun to stabilize
after six consecutive quarters of downgrades.

slowdown its pace of fiscal deficit reduction resulting in a further tailwind for sustained
growth momentum.

Economic and Market Dynamics
As India steadily progresses toward becoming the world’s third-largest economy, it
remains one of the few global markets offering the combination of scale, growth,
entrepreneurship, and improving institutional depth necessary for sustained
long-term wealth creation.
